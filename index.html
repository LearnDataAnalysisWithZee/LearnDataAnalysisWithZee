About Learn Data Analysis With Zee

The Learn Data Analysis with Zee initiative is focused on enlightening people about modern data work while staying grounded in the principles of traditional data analysis. It connects established analytical thinking with contemporary tools, practices, and ways of working, helping learners understand both the evolution of data analysis and its practical application today.

This initiative is free to access and is not registered, accredited, or formally certified. It exists purely in an educative spirit — to encourage curiosity, critical thinking, and skill development for anyone interested in learning more about data analysis, regardless of background or experience.

The material is intended to be used constructively: to learn, practise, reflect, and discuss. Learners are welcome to apply the ideas to their own projects and learning journeys, while using sound judgement and ethical practice.

In a friendly but professional spirit, the content should not be mis-used, misrepresented, or presented as original work. It is shared in the spirit of learn, practise, create - learn from this material, practise the ideas, and then go and build something of your own. Natural copyrights apply, as they do to all original educational material, maintaining the work remains respected, credited, and sustainable for the wider learning community.

State Led Conversation Script Extended Using If Then Logic

As a result of the fact find raw operational data is captured in a consistent and structured way. The fact find does more than record activity; rather provides the foundation for actionable insight. By analysing how quotes progress, how prices are delivered, and how customers respond at different stages, organisations can move beyond reporting and into purposeful conversational design. The insight generated from this data is used to design conversation scripts that actively support live interactions while a quote is being delivered, assisting consistency, clarity, and improved customer experience.

These insights lead to the creation of a state-led conversation script, designed to guide agents or advisors through live interactions in a structured yet flexible manner. Rather than prescribing rigid wording, the script defines conversational states and uses IF/THEN logic to adapt dynamically based on context, customer behaviour, and previously captured data. This approach supports both over-the-phone quote conversations and face-to-face quote processes, such as in-branch consultations, home visits, or on-site assessments, where the same conversational principles apply.

The first state in this model is Direction and Introduction. This state establishes context and sets the tone for the interaction. The direction of the interaction determines how the conversation begins, whether it is an inbound call from a customer requesting a quote, wether will start the quote process there and then or schedule it for later, then an outbound call as a scheduled callback, a follow‑up to correct or confirm details, or a conversation initiated to accommodate a change in customer requirements. In face‑to‑face settings, this state may involve confirming the purpose of a meeting, referencing a prior enquiry, or acknowledging previous discussions. By explicitly defining this state, the conversation starts with clarity, professionalism, and relevance, reducing friction and uncertainty from the outset.

The second state focuses on the conversation around Questions derived from the data source schema, most commonly a fact find. This is where structured information required for pricing and eligibility is captured. While the underlying questions are formal and often fixed, the conversation surrounding them is designed to be natural, explanatory, and customer‑focused. The script supports transitions between topics, explains why information is required, and adapts as did with the variations in introductions, using IF/THEN logic when certain answers trigger additional questions or alternative paths. This state applies equally to phone and face‑to‑face interactions, so that data is gathered accurately while maintaining rapport and trust.

The final state is What Happens Next, which brings the interaction to a clear and confident close. In this state, the agent or adviser explains the outcome of the conversation and sets expectations for next steps. This may include advising that a quote will be provided later, outlining a review or approval process, arranging a follow‑up call or meeting, or discussing whether additional products or services may be relevant. The customer is also given the opportunity to ask questions, so they leave the interaction informed and reassured. In face‑to‑face scenarios, this may also involve confirming documentation, timelines, or next visits, while on the phone it may include confirmation emails or scheduled callbacks.

By linking structured operational data to these three conversational states, organisations create a closed loop between data capture, performance insight, and live customer interaction. The fact find provides visibility into what is happening, while the state‑led conversation script determines how interactions ought to adapt in real time. This is so that both phone‑based and face‑to‑face quote processes are consistent, measurable, and continuously improved through data‑driven insight rather than static scripts.

“Consider you may be tired, due to a high volume of quotes on that day, however as far as the customer is concerned, it may well be their very first conversation for a quote that day, so maintaining a steady flow is essential by allowing the customer space during the conversation, therefore try not to rush the quote process.”

Conversation Script Design Prompt For Conversational Fact Find

You are a friendly, professional, and highly skilled customer-facing specialist.

Your task is to convert a structured fact find (a list of fields or required data points) into a natural, conversational script that feels like a real interaction—not a questionnaire.

This must be fully generalised and suitable for any industry (e.g. logistics, transport, finance, healthcare, recruitment, sales, etc.).

Core Objective: Transform structured questions into a smooth, human conversation that builds rapport, flows naturally, feels easy and comfortable for the client, and maintains professionalism and clarity.

Tone & Style: Keep the conversation warm, polite, and professional. Make it conversational rather than robotic, avoid repetitive phrasing, use natural acknowledgements, and ensure the language is clear and easy to follow.

Structure your output into 3 states:

  1. State 1 – Direction / Introduction

    Set the tone, explain the process, and guide the client. Include a friendly greeting, a brief introduction, a clear explanation of what will happen, reassurance that the process will be simple, and a request for permission to continue.

    Example style: “Hi, thanks for taking the time to speak with me today. I’ll just guide you through a few questions so I can get a clear picture and make sure we get the best outcome for you—does that sound okay?”

  2. State 2 – Questions

    Convert each fact find field into a natural, conversational question. Group related questions logically and use smooth transitions such as “To start with…”, “Just moving on to…”, or “Now, I’d like to ask about…”.

    Include light acknowledgements throughout the conversation such as “Perfect, thank you”, “That’s helpful”, or “Just to confirm…”. Keep the flow natural and avoid sounding like a checklist.

    Sensitive topics should be handled gently with reassuring language. Maintain a steady and engaging conversational rhythm.

  3. State 3 – What Happens Next

    Close the conversation clearly and professionally. Thank the client sincerely, explain the next steps, outline what they can expect, offer further help, and end on a positive note.

    Example style: “Thanks for going through all of that with me, I really appreciate it. I’ll now take everything we’ve discussed and move things forward. I’ll be in touch shortly with the next steps, and if you need anything in the meantime, just let me know.”

Fact Find Input: Insert the structured fields here (e.g. name, contact details, background, requirements, preferences, history, and any other relevant details).

Final Output Requirements: The script must read as if spoken by a real person, flow smoothly from start to finish, remain engaging and professional, and avoid robotic phrasing entirely.
